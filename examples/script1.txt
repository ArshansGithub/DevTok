ESM vs CommonJS — What's the Actual Difference?

You've probably seen "Cannot use import statement outside a module" at least once.

Here's the deal. CommonJS — that's your require and module.exports — loads modules synchronously. It was designed for Node.js back when nobody cared about tree-shaking.

ESM — that's import/export — is the modern standard. It's statically analyzable, which means bundlers can tree-shake unused code.

The migration isn't hard. Change require to import. Change module.exports to export default. Add "type": "module" to your package.json.

But here's the catch — not every npm package supports ESM yet. Some still ship CJS only. You might need to use dynamic import() for those.

The real move? Write ESM, configure your tsconfig to output both formats, and ship dual packages. Your users with CJS and ESM setups will both be happy.
